What this type of result enables

The purpose is not simply to count responses. The statistics can help identify which needs are most common, which schools require greater support and where facilitators and coordinators should focus.

Example: if digital assessment is consistently selected as a high need within a group of schools, facilitator support can prioritise practical digital assessment use at those schools instead of delivering a generalised intervention.

From Needs Analysis to Targeted Support

The Needs Analysis creates a structured link between district, school and educator-level information. This makes it possible to identify where particular needs are concentrated, understand which educators and schools require greater support, and direct interventions according to the evidence captured rather than applying the same support approach across every school.

How the information becomes useful

School selection creates the connection

When an educator selects a school, that school can be linked to its district and the responsible programme support structure. The results can therefore be analysed by school, educator, facilitator portfolio, coordinator portfolio and district. This allows the programme to see not only what the need is, but also where it is occurring and who is responsible for supporting that environment.

Programme outcome

Targeted intervention instead of generalised support

Facilitators can arrive at schools already aware of the dominant educator needs, while coordinators can identify which schools require greater attention or a shared intervention. Training, advocacy, resources and follow-up support can then be directed to the areas where the Needs Analysis shows the greatest requirement.
